How the ratings are calculated

This tool computes solunar activity directly from astronomy — the moon's and sun's real positions for your exact coordinates — the same underlying theory sites like Tides4Fishing build on. John Alden Knight's solunar theory (1926) holds that fish feed most during four daily windows: two major periods (~2 hrs, when the moon is directly overhead or directly underfoot) and two minor periods (~1 hr, at moonrise and moonset). Activity is thought to intensify further when a period overlaps sunrise or sunset, and around new and full moons when the sun and moon's pulls line up.

Each day's score sums its major and minor periods, weighted higher for majors, boosted for the sunrise/sunset overlap ("Prime"), and boosted again the closer the moon is to new or full. That produces the 0–5 rating you see on each day.

What's not included: actual tide height and current — whether water is rising, falling, high, or low at a specific harbor — depends on that location's measured harmonic tide constituents, not on formulas alone. To fold real tide times in, pull them from a source like NOAA CO-OPS (US) or a tide API for your region and merge them with the windows below. Weather, barometric pressure, and water temperature also swing results and aren't modeled here.

All astronomical positions use standard low-precision solar and lunar orbital formulas (Meeus / Schlyter), accurate to roughly a minute or two for sunrise/sunset and a few minutes for moon events — plenty for planning a trip, not survey-grade.
